Why do we need a season number?

What it isSome months are busy. Some are slow.

Why it mattersPlan a busy month with slow-month numbers and the target comes out too low.

An ice cream shop sells 100 cones a day in winter and 200 a day in summer. The owner plans summer using the winter average, so the plan says 100. Summer comes and the shop sells 200. The target was too easy.

In our salonDecember is busy. July is slow. The season number is the fix: it says how busy each month usually is.

Deep dive: what counts as sales

Services, treatments and courses, ex VAT, on days the branch traded. Treatments are already inside the Phorest "Services" and "Courses" lines, so nothing is missed. Retail is not in this number. Closed days are left out.

Deep dive: why the latest December is 2025

To find the normal level around a month we need 6 months of sales after it as well as 6 before. December 2026 has not happened yet, so December 2025 is the latest we can test. The method is the same for every month. The live plan in Piece 2 uses Aug, Sep and Oct 2026.

Why it mattersIt is what we divide by. counts half at each end (see deep dive).

Deep dive: why counts half

We want every month of the year to count exactly once. The 13 bars have twice, one at each end. If both counted in full, would be counted twice and pull the normal level towards it. So each end counts half: 0.5 + 0.5 = one . That is also why we divide by 12, not 13.

What you see
For each of her last 3 months: sales ÷ days she came in = her average sale on a day she comes in.

Why it mattersLeave and days off are never counted against her. Only days she worked.

Deep dive: which months count

Her sales here include treatments and courses, but not retail. A month counts if she worked 10 days or more. October counts at 6 days or more, because it is not finished. Part-timers count any month with days.

Her days, and her plan

What you seeHer days, then her day times her days.

Why it mattersThis is the number we write on her sheet.

Deep dive: how the days are chosen

The average days she worked in her full months (August and September). October is left out of this count because it is not finished. The most we plan is 26 days. Coach Emma can change a person's days.

Deep dive: the bottom-up check

The steps above are top-down: they only know the past. They cannot see who is on the floor this month. So before a plan is final, the branch manager and Mette ask one more question: given the real team (leave, a new starter with a guest book, a part-time rota), is this number too low or too high? If yes, a percentage is added or taken off that person's plan. Example: a plan that grows 7.3% on her normal day can become 17% for a stylist with a new guest book to fill. The adjustment is written next to the plan so everyone can see it.

What you seeThe three boxes from the top, now with real numbers.

Why it mattersHer normal day comes from her sales. The season number comes from the branches' history. One says who she is. The other says what kind of month is coming.

Deep dive: who is treated differently

Joiners have no history, so they get 70% of what the regulars in the same job plan for, or their own pace if that is lower. Leavers use the months they have (at least two). Anyone with fewer than two counted months is checked by Coach Emma first.
